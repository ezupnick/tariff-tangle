## Start screen
- **Headline:** Stock the season. / Beat the tariffs.
- **Intro:** When it comes to the holidays, the US and Canada build together, from the materials in a toy to the parts behind a string of lights. Follow those supply chains to fill your Holiday Cart with finished products. The catch: tariffs add to the price of everything you ship, so shop while working toward a US-Canada trade deal that removes those extra costs.
- **Supply-chain label:** From raw material to finished gift
- **Buttons:** Start the season · How to play
- **Product tags:** 🦃 Dinner · 🍪 Maple · 🎄 Tree · 🚗 Car · 💡 Lights · 🧸 Toys · 🎉 Party · 🕎 Menorah
- **How-to card 1:** **Match by color and dots** — Same-color tiles are one product's supply chain. 1 dot is a raw material, 2 dots a part. Match three 1-dot tiles to make a part, then match three 2-dot tiles of that color to ship the finished product. The two matches don't have to be back-to-back.
- **How-to card 2:** **Fill your Holiday Cart** — Ship the level's finished products before you run out of moves. Longer matches of 4 or 5 give bonus production and a free move.
- **How-to card 3:** **Tariffs and the trade deal** — Striped tariff tiles can't be matched or moved, and they add a charge to everything you ship, which raises holiday prices for families. Earn support by shipping and big matches, then sign a US-Canada trade deal to clear the tariffs.
- **Fine print:** Fact-based. The goods, routes and price effects are drawn from sources like the USDA, EIA, USTR, Statistics Canada, the Yale Budget Lab and the Tax Foundation. In-game prices are illustrative; the real figures appear on your end-of-level receipt.

## Tutorial pop-up (shown on first play or via "How to play")
**Screen 1 — The goal: Welcome to Tariff Tangle**  
You're stocking up for the US and Canada over the holiday season. Build raw materials into finished goods and ship them to your cart before your moves run out.  
_In real trade:_ Trade with Canada supports millions of US jobs, and the US sells more to Canada than to any other country.

**Screen 2 — Step 1: Swap and match 3**  
Tap a tile, then a neighbour, to swap them (or drag). Match three tiles of the same color and the same dots. Three 1-dot tiles merge into one 2-dot part.  
_In real trade:_ Real supply chains work this way: raw materials become parts, then finished products, often crossing the US-Canada border at each step.

**Screen 3 — Step 2: Build it, then ship it**  
To ship a finished product, match three 2-dot parts of the same color. Each part came from its own 1-dot match, so it takes a few matches, and they don't have to be back-to-back. Cascades often finish some for you.  
_In real trade:_ The US and Canada are each other's biggest customers, trading more than $2 billion in goods and services every day.

**Screen 4 — Why it matters: Every good crosses the border**  
The goods you build are made by both countries together. One car part can cross the US-Canada border four times before assembly. That is the real supply chain you're playing.  
_In real trade:_ About half the content of a car built in Canada actually comes from the United States.

**Screen 5 — Watch out: Tariffs tax everything you ship**  
Striped tariff tiles can't be swapped, matched, or cleared by playing. While any sit on the board, every good you ship pays a surcharge, so families pay more for the holidays. The only way to remove them is to reach a trade deal.  
_In real trade:_ A tariff is a tax collected at the border. It gets built into the price of goods, so families pay more for their holidays.

**Screen 6 — Your edge: Reach a Trade Deal**  
Each product you ship and each big match wins over one side: the US President, the Canadian Prime Minister, Congress and Parliament. When all four are on board, the Sign button lights up, click it to clear every tariff at once.  
_In real trade:_ In reality, bringing tariffs down takes an agreement that leaders and lawmakers in both countries support.

## Sources pop-up
- **Intro:** Every fact in the game is drawn from a public source. In-game prices and surcharges are illustrative, scaled from these ranges.
- **Holiday Dinner.** Canada supplies about 86% of US frozen fries and is the #2 US farm market. (USDA)
- **Maple & Baking.** Canada makes about 73% of the world's maple syrup, nearly all of it from Quebec. (Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada)
- **Tree & Trimmings.** Canada sends the US most of its fresh Christmas trees, and softwood lumber carries roughly a 35% duty. (Statistics Canada; US Commerce)
- **Car Under the Tree.** One auto part can cross the US-Canada border 4 times before assembly; a US-built car is about 50% US content. (NBC News; USTR)
- **Holiday Lights.** Canada supplies about 60% of US oil imports and powers the Northeast grid, with no tariff on energy today. (EIA)
- **Toys & Games.** Most toys are made overseas, so tariffs on toys and their parts push up the prices families pay over the holidays. (Peterson Institute)
- **New Year's Party.** Canada is the top supplier of paper and packaging to the US, the material behind party hats, streamers, wrapping and boxes. (US Census; UN Comtrade)
- **Hanukkah Lights.** Candles, menorahs and holiday decorations use imported wax, oil and metals, and tariffs on those inputs raise their prices. (US Census)
- **Household cost.** Tariffs cost the average US household about $1,000 to $1,800 a year (Tax Foundation, Yale Budget Lab, Peterson Institute, 2025-26).
- **Trade and jobs.** US-Canada trade totals and the jobs they support (US Census Bureau, trade.gov, US Chamber of Commerce, Statistics Canada).
